Convolutional Neural Network — a model that learns to recognize local patterns in images, from edges and shapes to entire objects.

1. How a CNN works

A CNN looks at small neighborhoods of pixels instead of treating the whole image as one unstructured list. It repeatedly extracts features and uses them to make a prediction.

5. How a CNN learns

During training, a CNN predicts an output, compares it with the correct answer, and adjusts its filter weights to reduce the error. This process repeats across many examples.

6. Three important CNN ideas

Local receptive field

A small filter inspects a local patch rather than the whole image at once.

Weight sharing

The same learned filter is reused in different locations, reducing parameters.

Stride & padding

Stride controls how far the filter moves; padding controls treatment of image borders.

Technical note: common CNN libraries perform cross-correlation (without flipping the kernel), though the operation is traditionally called “convolution.”

Remember: Convolution finds local patterns. ReLU adds nonlinearity. Pooling summarizes spatial features. Training learns which filters are useful.
